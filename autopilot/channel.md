## What this channel is about
Educational storytelling, with safety built in. Each video tells a short, true, gripping story or answers
a question people actually wonder about, and the viewer walks away knowing something useful.

Three kinds of video, mixed through the week:
1. **How your body works**: everyday mysteries explained plainly (what hiccups are, why we yawn,
   what goosebumps do), with a practical takeaway where there is one (when to see a doctor, what actually helps).
2. **True stories from history and science**: real people and discoveries told as a story, like Ötzi the Iceman,
   the 5,300-year-old man two hikers found frozen in the Alps. Hook, mystery, what scientists found out.
3. **Staying safe**: real risks explained calmly so viewers (and parents) know what to do. For example, how
   people get scammed or approached by strangers on games like Roblox, how to spot it, and what to do
   (block, report, tell a trusted adult). Online safety, home safety, outdoor safety, weather, first aid basics.

Audience: curious teens and adults, and parents watching with kids. Keep it family-friendly.
<!-- FOCUS END -->

## Rules (always apply)
- Made with the Permeable Framework: narrator voice over everything, 1-bit cream-and-ink look, Bellhead character.
- Every fact must be accurate and checkable. Follow docs/CHECKLIST.md. Debated ideas are framed as debated.
- Safety topics: calm and practical, never scary for the sake of it. Give clear steps (stop, block, report,
  tell a trusted adult, call emergency services). Never show or describe how to do the harmful thing itself.
  Name real platforms only for well-documented, general risks, and keep it fair.
- Nothing gory or graphic. Tension comes from the story, not violence.
- 35 to 70 seconds, 8 to 13 captions. Follow docs/STYLE.md. No title cards or end cards.
- Never repeat a topic or story part that's already in autopilot/history.jsonl. If a story series is running,
  continue it in order.
