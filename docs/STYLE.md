# Style rules

These keep every video in the series feeling like part of the same set.

## Look
- **Two colours**: cream `rgb(232,226,206)` on ink `rgb(6,6,9)`. Everything else is a shade between them, and the engine turns it into a 1-bit dither pattern automatically.
- **Red is only for villains**: bacteria, viruses and hostile cells. In the Ötzi video, the arrowhead was the one weapon and it was red. Blood, hearts, muscles and fire are never red, and neither are warnings.
- **Contrast**: neighbouring things need clearly different tones, or they melt together once dithered. If two areas read as the same grey, push one lighter and one darker.
- **Lines**: characters, clothes and props have a slightly wobbly, hand-drawn outline that redraws a few times a second ("boil"). It comes for free when you use the character.
- **No titles**: there are no title cards or end cards, and no logos. The video starts on the first image and fades out at the end.
- **Frame**: 9:16 vertical, with nothing important outside the frame. Keep key drawings between y 36 and 196 in the 180×320 world. The captions sit below that, and the app's buttons cover the bottom and right edges.

## Captions
- Captions are always in capitals, centred, and typed out word by word with the voice. They sit in a dark box inside the safe area.
- Use one or two short sentences each, with no more than four lines on screen.
- The pixel font has only `A–Z 0–9 . , ! ? ' - : / +`. Write "CO2" rather than "CO₂" and "OTZI" rather than "ÖTZI". `kit.py check` catches anything else.

## Tone
- Teach plainly and warmly. Don't use jokes, "POV" hooks, memes or "wait for it".
- Keep it atmospheric but quick: a new visual every few seconds.
- Be accurate and don't overstate. Debated ideas say so ("may", "one idea", "still debated"). Results from animal studies say which animal ("in mice").
- Topics can be serious, like loneliness or grief. Handle them gently, with no graphic detail.

## Characters
- The series character is Bellhead: a round head, a bell-shaped body and floating round hands. He can be the narrator-as-scientist, the subject of the story, or a crowd.
- You don't have to use him. Diagram-only videos like goosebumps work well.
- Animals in a character scene should look like Bellheads too. The chimps in the yawning video are smaller Bellheads with ears and a fur cap, not separate realistic drawings.

## Posting text
- A plain description of one or two sentences, with no emoji spam.
- Exactly five hashtags, including `#ai`.
